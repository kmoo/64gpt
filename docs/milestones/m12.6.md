# M12.6 — corpus density on the laggards: a fourth honest negative

**Status: LANDED (2026-07-26) as a code/test/corpus commit — NOT a
ROM/model release.** What shipped: the experiment (corpus expansion +
spike script + finding). What did NOT ship: a model that clears the
coherence gate, or any new `versions/` entry. Depends on M12.4
(`docs/milestones/m12.4.md`).

## What we tried

M12.5's own "For M12.6" section named corpus density on the laggards as
"still cheapest, still unclaimed": M12.4's two worst groups were guard
(1.75 inv/line) and korrath (2.00); under FiLM (M12.5) korrath got much
worse (3.62) while guard actually improved (0.88), meaning the two
laggards don't move together — worth measuring density directly instead
of guessing.

A rigorous (values-only, not dict-keys) phrase-bank count found korrath
and elowen running ~13-22% thinner than shadewrath despite equal
`PER_COMBO` in `make_m12_1_blob.py`:

| character | openers | bodies | closers |
|---|---|---|---|
| shadewrath (reference) | 86 | 128 | 44 |
| korrath (before) | 76 | 112 | 36 |
| elowen (before) | 80 | 112 | 36 |

Hand-wrote new lines reusing each character's *existing* vocabulary/
motifs (korrath: binding, years, duty, discipline; elowen: hope, home,
captivity, grace) rather than introducing new words — the char-level
GRU's alphabet doesn't change either way, but reusing known words in new
combinations increases exposure-per-word without diluting the training
signal with rare tokens. Result: korrath 86/128/45, elowen 90/128/45 —
now at or above shadewrath's density.

## The result: no better than M12.2, still short of M12.4

| | invented/line |
|---|---|
| M12.1 gate (target) | ≤ 1.00 |
| **M12.4 (redundancy-ablated, thin corpus)** | **1.44** |
| M12.6 (redundancy-ablated, densified korrath/elowen) | 1.65 |
| M12.2 (prefix-only, richer voices) | 1.65 |
| M12.5 (FiLM) | 2.15 |
| M12.3 (D:/M: in both prefix and columns) | 2.33 |

Per group: selena 1.38, guard 1.75, cast 1.12, shadewrath 1.38, korrath
1.25, elowen 3.00. Korrath actually improved slightly relative to
M12.4's 2.00, but **elowen got dramatically worse** (3.00 vs M12.4's
1.88) despite being one of the two densified characters — the opposite
of what the density hypothesis predicted for that group, and it dragged
the overall rate down to tie M12.2 exactly.

**Density (alone) was likely not the cause.** Adding well-integrated,
vocabulary-consistent bank entries — reused automatically across combos
by the existing template grammar, not one-off lines — did not close the
gap, and hurt the one character (elowen) it should have helped most
under the density story. This rules out "just write more lines in the
same style" as a fix, independent of conditioning mechanism (M12.4's
input-concat here; the same corpus feeds M12.1's plain prefix-only
mechanism too, untested on it directly since no new production build was
run — see "What did NOT happen" below).

## What DID land

- `korrath_corpus.py`, `princess_corpus.py` — density-expanded bank
  content (10 new openers, 16 new bodies, 9 new closers each), same
  template-grammar structure, no new vocabulary.
- `trainer/m12_6_density_spike.py` — host-only real-corpus spike, same
  discipline as M12.3/M12.4/M12.5 (fp32 train + int8 `quantize()`/
  `ref_impl`, no blob/kernel/ROM artifacts).
- `trainer` pytest green: 214 passed, 3 deselected (unchanged from
  M12.5 — pure corpus-content edits, no new/broken behavior needing
  tests).

## What did NOT happen (deliberately)

`korrath_corpus.py`/`princess_corpus.py` are the real production corpus
modules — `make_m12_1_blob.py` (the actual shipped-mechanism build
script) imports them directly, unlike the M12.3/M12.4/M12.5 spikes,
which touched only `model.py`/`ref_impl.py`/`quantize.py` and never the
corpus files themselves. That means this milestone's corpus edits are
now sitting in the real production input — but they were only ever
coherence-tested under M12.4's per-step conditioning mechanism, not
under M12.1's plain prefix-only mechanism that `make_m12_1_blob.py`
actually ships. M12.2 already showed that richer corpora regress raw
coherence under that exact plain mechanism (1.65 vs M12.1's ≤1.00), so
feeding an even-denser corpus into a real production build without
testing would risk a silent, unverified regression relative to the
already-shipped `m12_1_64gpt.z64`.

**No new production training run was started.** Re-running
`make_m12_1_blob.py` costs the same order of GPU time as this spike
(1-3+ hours) purely to find out whether density alone regresses the
*plain* mechanism too — a real question, but not free, and not worth
spending given density has now failed twice (M12.2's richer-voices
corpus, this milestone's targeted density) under two different
mechanisms. `versions/m12_1_64gpt.z64` (tag `m12.1`) remains the only
verified-good artifact and was not touched or rebuilt.

## For M13 (and beyond)

M12.4 (1.44, input-concat, redundancy-ablated, thin corpus) remains the
best result across this entire line. Three levers have now been tried
and none have cleared the ≤1.00 gate: two conditioning mechanisms
(M12.3's duplication, M12.5's FiLM) and one corpus lever (this
milestone's manual density increase). The manual-density result
specifically sharpens M13's own premise (`docs/milestones/m13.md`
mechanism 2): **raw bank-entry count, even when well-integrated and
vocabulary-consistent, is not the lever** — M13's actual bet (LLM-
proposed fragments passed through a structural/density gate and a
judge pass, validated against real training outcome before being
trusted) targets *quality and structural fit* of new fragments, not
just quantity, which this milestone's negative result now independently
supports rather than merely assumes. The `m12.3-voices` branch's fate
(merge as documented-negative history, or stay parked) is a decision
for whoever picks up M13 next — no further M12.x mechanism or corpus
attempt is recommended before that.
